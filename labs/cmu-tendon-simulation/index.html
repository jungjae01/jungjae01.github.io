Overview

This research investigates whether increasing the physical fidelity of tendon actuation models can reduce discrepancies between simulated and real tendon-driven robots. Many scalable simulation pipelines use simplified mappings from actuator commands to joint torques, which can omit routing friction, direction-dependent hysteresis, and other nonlinear transmission effects.

My work focuses on building a reusable GPU-accelerated simulation pipeline that places an explicit tendon-transmission layer between commanded tendon effort and the joint torques applied by the simulator. Using the ORCA robotic hand as an initial case study, I am implementing and evaluating route-level transmission models, comparing them with simplified baselines, and preparing the framework for later reinforcement-learning and sim-to-real experiments.
