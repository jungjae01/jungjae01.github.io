Bridging High-Fidelity Simulations and Physics-Based Learning using a Surrogate Model for Soft Robot Control

A unified sim-to-real framework connects real hardware, high-fidelity finite-element simulation, and an accelerated physics-based surrogate to enable reinforcement-learning control of a nonlinear pneumatic soft manipulator.

TL;DR

A calibrated SOFA finite-element model provides high-fidelity soft-body dynamics. A Transformer-based physics-informed model transfers those dynamics into a fast six-link PyBullet surrogate, where reinforcement-learning policies are trained and then deployed on the real pneumatic manipulator for trajectory tracking, force control, and hybrid position-force control.

Why Soft Robot Control Needs Two Levels of Simulation

Soft manipulators offer safe and adaptable interaction, but their nonlinear, hyperelastic deformation is difficult to model accurately. High-fidelity finite-element models can represent material behavior and contact in detail, yet their computational cost makes them unsuitable for reinforcement learning, which requires large numbers of environment interactions.

Training directly on hardware is also problematic. Extensive exploration can damage compliant actuators, data acquisition is slow, and experimental measurements contain noise and hardware-specific delays. The central challenge is therefore not simply to make simulation faster. It is to preserve the physical behaviors that matter for control while reducing the model to a form that can support accelerated policy learning.

The proposed framework addresses this problem by connecting three domains:

  1. A real pneumatic soft manipulator
  2. A calibrated high-fidelity SOFA FEM simulation
  3. A computationally efficient PyBullet surrogate environment

A Three-Domain Simulation-to-Learning Pipeline

The framework establishes mappings among real hardware, a high-fidelity FEM model, and an accelerated rigid-link surrogate. Each domain serves a different purpose.

The real robot provides measured configurations for calibration and final sim-to-real validation. The SOFA model captures nonlinear deformation, pressure actuation, and contact-related behavior without repeated mechanical wear. The PyBullet surrogate provides a fast environment compatible with standard reinforcement-learning tools.

Data-driven mappings connect pressure commands, state representations, and surrogate joint configurations across these domains. This separation makes it possible to retain high-fidelity physical knowledge during model construction while performing large-scale policy training in a substantially faster environment.

Real Robot

  • Three independently pressure-actuated bellows
  • Motion-capture state estimation
  • Load cell and interchangeable tool tips
  • Source of calibration and sim-to-real measurements

SOFA FEM Simulation

  • Volumetric tetrahedral mesh
  • Three internal pressure cavities
  • Hyperelastic material representation
  • External force and contact modeling
  • High-fidelity data source

PyBullet Surrogate

  • Six stacked rigid planar links
  • Rotational and prismatic joints
  • FEM-derived range of motion and contact dimensions
  • High-rate simulation for RL training

Calibrating the FEM Model Against Hardware

The physical platform is a bellow-type parallel manipulator with three independently controlled pneumatic chambers positioned between a fixed base and a movable top plate. Its configuration is represented using the tool-center-point position, backbone length, curvature, and top-plate orientation.

The corresponding SOFA model uses a volumetric tetrahedral mesh with internal cavity meshes for pressure actuation. Material and hyperelastic parameters are optimized to align simulated deformation with real motion-capture measurements.

A multilayer perceptron maps real pressure commands to the calibrated simulation pressure domain. The real-data collection process used 5,000 distinct pressure configurations, while the learned real-to-simulation mapping achieved an average pressure error of 0.13 ± 0.02 kPa.

Model-Order Reduction

Proper orthogonal decomposition reduces the FEM model while retaining dominant deformation modes. Time-series nodal states are assembled into a snapshot matrix, decomposed using singular value decomposition, and projected onto a lower-dimensional basis.

p(t) ≈ Phi a(t)

Here, p(t) is the time-varying deformation state, Phi is the reduced basis, and a(t) contains the reduced coordinates.

More than 100,000 simulation frames were used during reduced-order snapshot generation. Critical locations, including the tool-center-point node and boundary contact nodes, were retained so that the reduced state continued to represent position, curvature, arc length, orientation, and contact-relevant geometry.

Learning Soft-Body Dynamics from FEM Data

The dynamics model predicts the manipulator's next Cartesian velocity from a time sequence containing the current FEM state, pneumatic pressure commands, and external tool-center-point forces.

A Transformer encoder processes the temporal input sequence. The architecture uses two attention layers with four attention heads per layer. Its latent representation is passed to a physics-informed neural network that predicts the next velocity state.

Training combines two objectives:

  1. A supervised prediction loss against SOFA-generated state velocities
  2. A physics residual derived from constrained multibody dynamics

Total loss = prediction loss + lambda x physics-residual loss

The data term rewards agreement with the FEM dataset, while the residual term discourages predictions that violate the modeled mass, elastic, damping, pressure, and constraint relationships.

Approximately two million FEM samples were used to train the dynamics model. The learned forward model achieved:

  • Position error: 3.35 ± 0.43 mm
  • Velocity error: 14.56 ± 4.38 mm/s

A Contact-Aware Physics-Based Surrogate

The high-fidelity FEM model is too computationally expensive for reinforcement-learning rollout. The accelerated surrogate therefore represents the manipulator using six vertically stacked planar links.

Adjacent links are connected by:

  • Two rotational joints for omnidirectional bending
  • One prismatic joint for axial extension

The surrogate geometry is not arbitrary. Boundary nodes extracted from the reduced FEM mesh are projected onto layer-wise regression planes. Their radial distributions define conservative collision boundaries and guide the link dimensions.

The model is implemented in URDF and includes mass distributions, joint limits, range-of-motion constraints, and dimensions derived from the FEM representation. This provides a reduced rigid-link abstraction that preserves the dominant deformation and contact characteristics needed for policy training.

The PyBullet environment runs at more than 1000 Hz.

Mapping Pressure Actuation into Joint Space

The FEM model is actuated by three pneumatic pressure inputs, while the surrogate is controlled through multiple joint coordinates. A constrained optimization aligns the surrogate tool-center-point position with FEM-generated target states while respecting joint limits.

L-BFGS-B is used to solve the constrained configuration-matching problem. The resulting optimized pressure-to-joint pairs train a multilayer perceptron that provides continuous actuation-space mapping.

Reported mapping errors:

  • Rotational joints: 0.32 ± 0.02 degrees
  • Prismatic joints: 4.23 ± 0.06 mm

Because the manipulator is kinematically redundant, multiple joint configurations can produce similar tool-center-point positions. Joint-level errors should not be interpreted as direct end-effector errors.

From Desired Motion to Pneumatic Commands

The inverse model estimates the pressure input required to produce a desired state transition. Its inputs include the current configuration, external contact force, and target Cartesian velocity.

A multilayer perceptron predicts the pressure command. A cycle-consistency loss passes this prediction through the pretrained forward model, encouraging the inferred pressure to reproduce the desired motion.

Reported inverse-model errors:

  • Pressure prediction: 0.38 ± 0.05 kPa
  • Reconstructed joint angle: 0.17 ± 0.04 degrees

Customized Reinforcement-Learning Environment

A Gymnasium-compatible environment wraps the PyBullet surrogate and exposes conventional observation, action, and reward interfaces.

Observation

  • Surrogate joint angles
  • Joint velocities
  • Tool-center-point position
  • Tool-center-point velocity
  • External contact force

Action

Continuous joint-level commands for the surrogate model.

Three learning methods were evaluated: Soft Actor-Critic, Proximal Policy Optimization, and Behavior Cloning. For each task, the policy with the highest final reward was selected for evaluation.

Task 1: Trajectory Tracking

The agent follows predefined Cartesian trajectories, including circular and star-shaped paths.

Task 2: Force-Induced Deformation Estimation

The agent estimates the manipulator's deformed position under an applied external load.

Task 3: Hybrid Position-Force Control

The agent follows a tilted elliptical trajectory on a sloped surface while maintaining a target normal contact force.

Reward Explanation

R = alpha Rq + (1 - alpha) RF

The reward combines normalized position and force terms. The parameter alpha controls the balance between geometric trajectory accuracy and contact-force regulation.

Transferring Learned Policies to the Real Robot

Policies are trained using joint-level commands in the PyBullet surrogate. Learned mappings translate those commands into the pressure domains used by the high-fidelity model and the physical pneumatic system.

A moving-average filter smooths the resulting pressure sequence before hardware execution. Policy inference runs at more than 300 Hz, but the physical robot is operated at approximately 2 Hz because of pneumatic hardware response limitations.

The experimental setup mirrors geometry, contact surfaces, and force-measurement instrumentation between simulation and hardware. This consistency supports direct policy transfer without retraining on the real robot.

Within the reported scope, zero-shot deployment means that the trained policy is deployed on hardware without additional task-specific real-world policy training.

Generalization Beyond the Training Tasks

Unseen 3D Trajectory

The learned policy was evaluated on a nonplanar figure-eight trajectory on a curved surface that was not used during training. The real robot achieved an average tracking error of 5.34 ± 0.38 mm.

This experiment demonstrates transfer to a more complex spatial trajectory rather than only the planar paths used in the primary evaluation.

Recovery from a Pneumatic Disturbance

During circular trajectory tracking, the air supply to one pneumatic chamber was intentionally disconnected and restored five seconds later. The controller deviated from the reference path during the disturbance and returned to the trajectory within approximately five seconds after reconnection.

This result is evidence of closed-loop recovery, not complete disturbance rejection.

Hardware Bandwidth

The policy inference and surrogate simulation rates are substantially faster than the physical actuator response. Tracking error increased sharply when pressure command frequency exceeded approximately 2 Hz, with measured position error rising from 13.8 mm to 63.9 mm.

The physical pneumatic system, rather than the policy inference loop, is therefore the primary rollout-speed bottleneck in the reported setup.

Limitations and Future Work

The rigid-link surrogate retains the dominant range of motion and contact geometry required for learning, but it does not explicitly reproduce distributed elasticity or full soft-body interaction. Contact-rich scenarios may therefore expose discrepancies that are not present in the reduced representation.

The inverse mapping is deterministic and may become less reliable under uncertain loading, sensor noise, or behavior outside the training distribution. Real hardware also introduces effects such as pneumatic delay, contact hysteresis, and material variability.

Future extensions include:

  • Pseudocontinuum or compliant-joint surrogate elements
  • Higher-fidelity contact and tactile feedback
  • Contact-rich manipulation
  • Locomotion
  • Interaction with deformable environments
  • Probabilistic or uncertainty-aware inverse models
  • Extension to fiber-reinforced, tendon-driven, and other actuator types

Toward Fast, Quantitative Learning for Soft Robots

The framework separates physical fidelity from training speed without treating them as unrelated systems. Real measurements calibrate the high-fidelity FEM model, FEM data train physics-informed dynamics, and the resulting knowledge is transferred into an accelerated surrogate suitable for reinforcement learning.

Across trajectory, deformation, hybrid-control, generalization, and disturbance experiments, the framework demonstrates a practical path from computationally expensive soft-body simulation to quantitative real-world control.
