Engineering Project

Analysis of the Force Exerted by an Elevator

Measured how elevator acceleration changes the force experienced by a smartphone, then used MATLAB to compare three elevators in Boston University’s Warren Towers.

Report
Context
Boston University, ENG EK 125 Programming
Timeline
Sep 2021 - Dec 2021
  • MATLAB
  • Data Analysis
  • Experimental Measurement
  • Newton’s Second Law

Overview

This project investigated the relationship between acceleration and force during everyday elevator travel. Acceleration data was collected with a smartphone in three elevators at Boston University’s Warren Towers, providing a small comparative dataset from real operating systems.

The measurements were processed in MATLAB to identify the largest and smallest recorded accelerations for each elevator. Newton’s second law was then used to translate the measured motion into the force experienced by the phone.

Method

A smartphone served as the test object and motion sensor during rides in Elevators 1, 2, and 3. For each elevator, the analysis focused on the acceleration extrema associated with starting, stopping, and changes in direction.

MATLAB organized the measurements by elevator, extracted the maximum and minimum acceleration values, plotted the comparison, and calculated force from the phone’s mass and measured acceleration.

Results

The three elevators produced closely grouped results. The calculated forces exerted on the phone were 2.10 N, 2.12 N, and 2.11 N for Elevators 1, 2, and 3, respectively.

Maximum measured accelerations clustered near 10.9 m/s², while minimum values were approximately 8.8–9.0 m/s². These departures from gravitational acceleration capture the brief “heavier” and “lighter” sensations that occur as an elevator accelerates and decelerates.

Takeaways

The near-identical force estimates suggest broadly consistent performance across the three Warren Towers elevators. More importantly, the project connected a familiar physical experience to a complete data-analysis workflow: collecting sensor measurements, isolating meaningful extrema, visualizing results, and applying a physics model in MATLAB.
