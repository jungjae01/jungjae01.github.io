Research Poster

A minimally invasive soft robotic device designed to provide controllable partial-to-complete aortic occlusion through deployable soft balloons and an internal stabilization mechanism.

Overview

Emergency aortic occlusion can reduce blood loss from non-compressible torso hemorrhage, but complete occlusion may cause vessel injury, ischemia, and reperfusion complications. This project investigates a minimally invasive soft robotic device intended to provide adjustable occlusion rather than a fixed binary blockage.

How It Works

  1. 1

    Deliver

    The device is compressed for insertion through an approximately 5 mm access port.

  2. 2

    Stabilize

    A deployable internal bracing structure expands within the vessel model to support the occlusion balloons under fluid flow.

  3. 3

    Regulate

    Soft balloons are inflated from a deflated state through partial inflation to full occlusion, allowing aortic pressure to be adjusted through balloon pressure.

Preliminary Results

5 mm

Approximate access-port diameter

30 mm

Maximum deployed device diameter

R² = 0.978

Reported proximal-pressure linear fit

Initial in vitro testing demonstrated variable occlusion in a simplified aortic flow model. The phased-tetracuspid balloon configuration showed a strong linear relationship between inflation pressure and measured proximal aortic pressure.

Stabilization and Integration

Balloon testing without a stabilization mechanism made deployment difficult in the presence of fluid flow. A rigid pop-up bracing mechanism fabricated using laminate and MEMS-inspired techniques was therefore developed to support the soft occlusion elements.

Current Scope

The reported results are preliminary and were obtained in a simplified in vitro aortic model. The poster identifies further testing, simulation, closed-loop feedback integration, and ex vivo and in vivo evaluation as future development.
