Overview

The Tractive System Active Lamp, or TSAL, is a safety indicator designed to communicate when a Formula Electric vehicle’s high-voltage tractive system is active. For the 2022 vehicle, the lamp was required to operate directly from the tractive system and flash continuously within the competition-defined frequency range.

The project translated that safety requirement into a compact, hardware-based electrical subsystem without software-dependent control.

System Architecture

An astable 555 timer circuit generates the periodic signal for a 12 V red beacon intended for the vehicle’s roll hoop. A DC/DC converter supplies the low-voltage circuit from the tractive system, while a transistor switching stage allows the timing circuit to control the external lamp.

The resistor and capacitor values were selected for a target flashing frequency of approximately 2.32 Hz.

Development

Development progressed from rule analysis and circuit calculations to simulation and breadboard prototyping. The validated circuit concept then informed component selection, schematic capture, and PCB layout in KiCad.

  1. Interpret the competition safety and flashing requirements.
  2. Analyze and simulate the astable 555 timer circuit.
  3. Build a breadboard prototype and evaluate the timing behavior.
  4. Select components for the timing, conversion, and switching stages.
  5. Translate the circuit into a schematic and compact PCB layout.
